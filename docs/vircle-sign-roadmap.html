Roadmap: from core signing to merchant go-live and beyond

Week 0 is the day you give the go-ahead. The product is built in four phases behind an operator switch, first for Vircle only. Each phase ends with something you can use and a gate you can check. The plan counts 116 working days of build (23 weeks) plus a 3-day start-up.

Merchant go-live: about week 13.6

That is the end of Phase 1B: about 68 working days, roughly 3 months. With two engineers working in parallel it is about 8 to 9 weeks.

Until then an agent can already send the merchant application as a document the merchant fills on the page.

What you get, phase by phase

Phase 1A · weeks 0 to 9.2

Core signing

46 days

Upload a PDF, Word file or image, place signing locations, text, dates and numbers, list signers with an optional signing order, send by email or WhatsApp, sign on a phone, sealed PDF filed on the contact. Categories and the add-on mechanism.

Gate: a Word file is signed on an Android phone and an iPhone and verifies in a PDF reader.
Phase 1B · weeks 9.2 to 13.6

Forms and merchant groups

22 days

The merchant application filled in parts from one link with autosave, conditional fields and uploads, the e-invoice tax details, and variants per merchant group A, B and C. The Merchant Registration add-on goes live.

Gate: a merchant starts on a phone, finishes on a computer through the same link, and signs.
Phase 2 · weeks 13.6 to 19.8

Automate and scale

31 days

Automatic onboarding from a contact tag, webhooks and public API, reminders that name unfinished parts, countersign inside Halo, share a part with a colleague, option lists, envelopes, bulk send.

Gate: a tagged contact goes to a filed, signed agreement with no manual step.
Phase 3 · weeks 19.8 to 23.2

Trust and compliance

17 days

Public registration entry, certificate-authority signature, verify page, retention rules, form without signature, load test and security review.

Gate: sealed files show as trusted; 200 documents in an hour complete in time.

Where it can slip

Critical path. Foundation, PDF engine, send and sign, then the form work, then the merchant content. Conversion, wording and the management screens can run beside them.
Biggest unknowns. How closely Word files convert (fonts), how long the certificate takes, the VPS headroom, and whether merchant groups differ in clauses as well as fees. Each has an early check: a real-file conversion test in WP3, the certificate order in week 0, a measurement in WP2, and your answer on groups before WP12.

Scope guardrails

Not planned: signing in person on a shared device, SMS delivery, Excel and PowerPoint input, face or ID-card verification, editing the text of a document inside the product, and a customer-facing launch. The last is a switch and pricing decision after Vircle uses it, not new build.
