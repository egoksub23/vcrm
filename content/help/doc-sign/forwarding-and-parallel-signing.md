Two things in Secure Sign help when the right person is not the first one to open the link:

- **Signing together.** People who share a step sign at the same time, instead of one after another.
- **Forwarding.** A signer hands their turn, or one part of a form, to a colleague, and you can see exactly who did what.

## Sign together (steps)

Turn on **This document needs signing order** on the **People** step. Each person has a **step number**. Give two people the same number and they are one **step**: they are both invited when the step begins, and the next step begins only when everyone in the step has finished.

1. Add the people and tick **This document needs signing order**.
2. Type a step number for each person. People with the same number are shown together under a heading such as **Step 1 (2 people)**.
3. To change the order, drag a person next to another or use the arrows. Dragging or using an arrow puts the person in a step of their own, just before or after the one next to them.

What it means in practice:

- **Everyone in a step is invited together**, when the step begins. The first step is invited when you send.
- **The next step waits for all of them.** If one person has signed and the other has not, the next step stays closed.
- **A decline by anyone stops everything.** It stops the whole document, as before.
- **Reminders go only to the people whose step it is.** A person in a later step is not reminded, and has no link yet.
- **The expiry date is for the whole document.** It is not per step.
- **A person who has signed sees who they are waiting for.** If three people share a step, the page names the ones who have not finished.

The history says why each person was invited: "Siti was invited because Ali finished", or, when a step had several people, "Siti was invited because the previous step finished". The sealed PDF lists the signers in step order, with the people who signed together side by side.

> [!NOTE]
> Without signing order there are no steps: everyone is invited at once.

### After you have sent it

On the **People** tab of a sent document:

- A person whose step has not begun can be **renamed or given another email address** with **Change recipient**. Nothing is sent to them until their step begins.
- A person whose step has not begun can be **moved to a later step** (or to a new last step) with **Move to**. A step that has already begun cannot be moved into.
- A person who has already been invited, or who has finished, can only be replaced with **Change recipient**, which gives the new person a new link in the same place.
- The signing order itself cannot be switched on or off after sending. Void the document and send it again.

## Forward a turn or a part

A signer can hand over their work:

- **The whole turn.** The new person becomes the signer for that role, in the same place in the order. They agree to sign electronically for themselves, review and sign. The signer's own link stops working at once.
- **One part of a form.** The signer keeps the signing role and hands one part to a colleague, for example the bank details to the finance team. That person fills in only that part and never signs.

### Switch it on

Forwarding is **off unless you switch it on**.

- **For a template:** in the template's options, tick **Allow forwarding**. Documents made from it start with forwarding on.
- **For one document:** tick **Allow people to forward** in the options step before you send, or on the document's page while it is open. You can switch it off again at any time. People who already received something keep it.

### What the signer does

When forwarding is on, the signer's page shows **Forward to someone else**. For a form, each part that is not finished also shows **Forward this part to someone else**.

1. They enter the other person's name and email address, and can add a short note.
2. The other person gets an email with a link of their own, saying who asked them. The note is included.
3. For a whole turn, the signer's page then says it was forwarded and that they can close it. For a part, the part shows **Waiting for** the person, and the signer can **take it back** until it is completed. What the other person already typed stays.

### What the other person sees

- **A turn that was forwarded.** The full document, as the signer would have seen it. They give their own consent and sign.
- **A part that was forwarded.** Only that part of the form. They do not see the pages, the other parts, or who else is on the document. They cannot decline the document, pass the part on, or sign. When they press **Submit**, the part is done and the signer sees **Done by** their name.

The signer who handed over a part cannot finish while the part is out. They wait for it, or take it back.

### The limits

- A person can forward their position **at most two times** (a turn and a part both count). This stops a document being passed around.
- The address must be someone else: not the person's own, and not someone already on the document for that role. When the document needs signing order, it cannot be anyone already on the document.
- A person who was handed a part cannot hand it on.
- A signer cannot forward after they have signed.
- Forwarding does not count as a new document in your monthly limit.

### What you see

- **You are told.** When a signer forwards their turn, you get a notification in Halo and an email.
- **The People tab** shows who holds each position, "Forwarded from" the earlier names, and a person who was handed a part under the signer who gave it.
- **The Progress tab** of a form shows, for each part, who holds it and whether they have completed it.
- **The history** records every forward and every part taken back, with the names and a hidden email address (never the link). The sealed certificate names the person who did each thing at the time.
- A person who was handed only a part does not get the signed copy of the whole document by email. You and the signers do.

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting a step to start when one person has finished.** The next step waits for everyone in the step before it.
- **Giving two people the same number when the order matters.** The same number means they sign at the same time, so neither sees what the other entered first.
- **Switching forwarding on for a document that must be signed by a named person.** A forwarded turn makes the new person the signer. Leave forwarding off when it must be that one person.
- **Looking for a forward option on a person who has not been invited yet.** Only someone whose link is open can forward. Until then, use **Change recipient**.
